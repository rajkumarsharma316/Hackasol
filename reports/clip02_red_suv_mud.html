5. Method and limitations

Pipeline. Frames are sampled evenly; vehicles detected with YOLO11 and tracked by IoU; the plate is localised with a CRAFT text detector plus a plate-colour heuristic and a temporal-consistency filter; each plate crop is read by a CRNN recogniser whose full per-character CTC probabilities are kept; readings are aligned across frames and pooled with quality weights. Colour comes from k-means clustering of the vehicle body; body type fuses YOLO class votes with CLIP zero-shot classification; make/model is a CLIP zero-shot estimate.

Matching (TRACE). Every recovered character, colour and body type becomes a likelihood ratio against a random vehicle using visual-confusion models (8↔B, 0↔O↔D, 5↔S, white↔silver, car↔SUV ...). Plates are aligned with semi-global dynamic programming so partial and shifted reads work. Unknown characters are neutral (LR = 1). Scores are converted to posteriors including a 'not in registry' hypothesis; a match is declared only when the top posterior ≥ 50% and the plate evidence alone is substantial.

Limitations. Posteriors assume the sample registry is the population; colour is affected by lighting and casts; make/model is indicative only. Results are investigative leads and must be verified by an officer.
